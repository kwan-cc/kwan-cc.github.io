2026 US rPET Market Trends: What Procurement Managers Must Know Now

Mar 27, 2026

As a procurement manager responsible for sourcing rPET for US brands or packaging operations, you’re navigating record import levels, domestic plant closures, evolving tariffs, and tightening state regulations. Understanding these shifts is critical for securing stable supply, controlling costs, and ensuring compliance through 2027.

This guide summarizes the latest March 2026 insights from NAPCOR’s 2024 PET Recycling Report (released December 2025), the US-Taiwan Reciprocal Trade Agreement (February 2026), and current industry projections — with practical takeaways to strengthen your sourcing strategy.

Short-Term Opportunity: Taiwanese rPET Dominates Amid Domestic Shortages

Imported recycled PET (rPET) reached record levels in 2024, accounting for nearly 25% of total North American supply. Domestic reclamation capacity continues to shrink, with multiple major US plants closing or scaling back in 2025 due to intense price competition from lower-cost imports.

For procurement teams, this creates a clear near-term advantage:

  • Taiwanese suppliers offer high-quality, consistent food-grade rPET flakes, pellets, and sheets that many domestic sources struggle to match in volume and traceability.
  • The February 2026 US-Taiwan Reciprocal Trade Agreement establishes a 15% tariff framework, providing relative stability and competitive access compared to broader global tariff pressures.

This import reliance has helped brands meet sustainability targets while domestic supply lags, particularly on the West Coast.

Procurement Takeaway: Secure 6–12 month forward contracts with reliable Taiwanese suppliers immediately. Prioritize partners with full chain-of-custody documentation to support brand audits and upcoming EPR reporting requirements.

Long-Term Challenges: Rising Protectionism and Regulatory Pressure

NAPCOR has highlighted the strain on domestic infrastructure and called for measures to support US recycling capacity. While overall rPET consumption showed modest growth projections, the heavy reliance on imports risks further plant closures and supply instability.

Key Regulatory Developments

  • California’s SB 54 (Plastic Pollution Prevention and Packaging Producer Responsibility Act), with updated regulations advancing in early 2026 and full producer responsibility elements rolling out in 2027.
  • Continued emphasis on source reduction, recyclability mandates, and increased use of post-consumer recycled content.
  • Potential for stricter import oversight, enhanced traceability rules, and tariff adjustments under “America First” priorities.

West Coast shortages may persist even as national import volumes remain high.

Procurement Takeaway: Develop a diversified sourcing strategy now. Maintain core Taiwanese volume while actively qualifying US or near-shore reclaimers. Evaluate technology licensing or joint-venture opportunities with Taiwanese partners to reduce future quota and tariff exposure.

Machinery Demand: A Strategic Opportunity for Efficiency and Compliance

Plant closures and Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) investments are driving strong demand for advanced recycling equipment. The US PET recycling machinery market is projected to grow significantly, reaching approximately $1.2 billion by 2033 at a CAGR of around 10.2%, powered by needs for efficient sorting, washing, and food-grade pelletizing lines.

Procurement managers can leverage this trend by:

  • Investing in or recommending energy-efficient systems that help converters boost recycled content without relying solely on new domestic capacity.
  • Notable example: Solutions like Aceretech’s safPET food-grade pelletizing system, which integrates flake-to-pellet processing with lower energy consumption and faster ROI.

Procurement Takeaway: Include advanced recycling machinery in your 2026 capital planning. Equipment that delivers verified food-grade certification and reduced operating costs can serve as both a risk hedge and a value-add when negotiating with suppliers and brands.

March 2026 Latest Developments

  • NAPCOR continues to flag West Coast shortages and the displacement effect of record imports on domestic reclaimers.
  • California SB 54 regulations remain in active revision, with additional public comment periods concluded in early 2026 and implementation milestones approaching in 2027.
  • The US-Taiwan 15% reciprocal tariff framework is now in effect, offering a degree of predictability for Taiwanese material and technology flows.

These trends point to a structural rebalancing: short-term reliance on high-quality imports, paired with accelerating pressure to strengthen domestic and localized solutions.

5 Immediate Actions for Procurement Managers in Q2 2026

  1. Review and update supplier traceability documentation from all Asian sources.
  2. Model cost scenarios for potential tariff or quota changes beyond the current 15% framework.
  3. Qualify at least one additional US reclaimer and schedule technical reviews with Taiwanese machinery providers.
  4. Calculate ROI on energy-efficient pelletizing equipment to support internal capital requests.
  5. Align internal reporting processes with upcoming SB 54 and brand-specific PCR disclosure needs.

Taiwanese rPET and recycling technology still deliver clear advantages in quality, cost, and performance — but only for teams that move quickly to balance current opportunities with long-term resilience.

The market is evolving rapidly. Use the insights and checklist above to strengthen your position in 2026 and beyond.
