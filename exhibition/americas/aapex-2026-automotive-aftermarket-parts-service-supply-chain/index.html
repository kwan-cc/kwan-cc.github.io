AAPEX 2026: Automotive Aftermarket Parts, Service & Supply Chain

Aug 25, 2026

AAPEX 2026 returns to Las Vegas from November 3 to 5, 2026, bringing the automotive aftermarket to The Venetian Expo and the new AAPEX Forum. The event expects more than 2,500 exhibitors across 5,500 booths, with approximately 550,000 square feet devoted to more than 1,400 product categories. Unlike consumer-facing automotive events, AAPEX is restricted to industry professionals and is built around the commercial aftermarket, from component manufacturing and distribution to retail, repair and fleet service.

Those numbers make AAPEX one of the industry's major annual meeting points, but its relevance in 2026 goes beyond scale. North American aftermarket companies are dealing with aging vehicles, changing sourcing strategies, higher price sensitivity, technician shortages and increasingly complex repair requirements. That combination is making supplier evaluation and technical capability more closely connected than they were only a few years ago.

A Growing Aftermarket Keeps Procurement on the Agenda

The U.S. aftermarket enters AAPEX 2026 with relatively strong underlying demand. The latest Joint Channel Forecast from the Auto Care Association and MEMA Aftermarket Suppliers projects the U.S. light-duty automotive aftermarket to grow 5.2% in 2026. MEMA's forecast materials put the market at roughly $467 billion this year.

Vehicle demographics provide part of the explanation. S&P Global Mobility reported that the average age of U.S. light vehicles reached 12.8 years in 2025, while the number of light vehicles in operation increased to about 289 million. Older vehicles typically require more maintenance and replacement components, supporting demand across categories such as underhood parts, undercar components, fluids, diagnostics and shop equipment.

For AAPEX buyers, however, market growth does not eliminate purchasing pressure. An AAPEX survey of 448 North American aftermarket professionals conducted in early 2026 found that 53% had observed growing demand for lower-cost parts and services. At the same time, respondents still identified quality as a leading influence on customer preference.

That tension between affordability and product performance is likely to shape many supplier discussions in Las Vegas.

The Show Floor Is Becoming a Procurement Map

One of the more significant changes at AAPEX is organizational rather than technological.

AAPEX began grouping exhibitors by broad product and service categories in 2025 after buyers asked for a more efficient way to compare suppliers. For 2026, the organizers have extended the approach through a redesigned floor plan and the new AAPEX Forum, allowing exhibitors within the same category to remain together rather than being split into overflow areas.

This matters at a show of AAPEX's size. Category-based organization makes it easier for distributors, retailers and repair businesses to move directly between competing suppliers and compare product ranges, positioning and capabilities within a limited number of days.

The AAPEX Forum will concentrate Accessories and Lighting and Undercar exhibitors. The Venetian Expo's Level 2 will house categories including Diagnostics, Telematics and Shop Management Systems; EV and Alternative Fuel; Tools and Equipment; Tire Servicing; Underhood; Paint and Body; Remanufacturing; HVAC and Diesel Emissions; and Oil, Lube, Waxes and Chemicals. International sourcing and international pavilions are scheduled for Level 1.

The layout effectively mirrors the aftermarket supply chain rather than treating thousands of booths as a single general marketplace.

Supplier Diversification Is Moving From Strategy to Purchasing

Supply chain resilience should be another prominent theme around AAPEX 2026.

In the event's 2026 State of the Automotive Aftermarket Survey, 70% of respondents said supplier diversification had either been completed, was being planned or was already in progress. Post-show research from AAPEX 2025 also found that attendees ranked supply chain disruptions and tariffs among their leading concerns heading into 2026.

This gives the show's international sourcing component added relevance. AAPEX reported exhibitors from 43 countries in 2025, while its broader buyer audience regularly includes participants from well over 100 countries.

For importers and distributors, the conversation is therefore likely to extend beyond finding the lowest quotation. Buyers increasingly need to consider manufacturing geography, tariff exposure, inventory availability, lead times, quality consistency and the feasibility of maintaining alternative sources.

For international suppliers trying to enter or expand in North America, that creates an opening, but also raises the standard expected of prospective vendors. Competitive pricing alone is unlikely to be sufficient where buyers are actively reducing supply chain risk.

Service Technology Is Now Part of the Product Conversation

AAPEX also differs from a conventional parts sourcing exhibition because repair capability has become a substantial part of the event.

Joe's Garage provides operating service bays for demonstrations, while adjacent product sections cover diagnostics, telematics, shop management, tire service, tools, EVs and alternative-fuel vehicles. The 2026 program also includes training related to diagnostics, ADAS, EV servicing, workforce development and shop management.

This reflects a broader shift in aftermarket purchasing. A replacement component may now require calibration procedures, diagnostic software, vehicle data access or specialized equipment before a technician can complete the job correctly. As a result, distributors and repair businesses increasingly evaluate not only the physical part, but also the technical support and service process surrounding it.

AAPEX's mix of component suppliers, equipment companies, software providers and repair professionals makes those relationships more visible.

Preventive Maintenance Gets More Space in 2026

A new addition this year is iFLEX at AAPEX, developed with the Preventative Automotive Maintenance Association. It expands representation for oil, lubrication and quick-lube businesses while adding targeted education and industry networking.

The move is notable because electrification discussions have sometimes overshadowed the continuing economic importance of conventional vehicle maintenance. AAPEX's own 2026 research shows a mixed investment outlook for EV-related business, with more respondents planning to reduce EV investment than increase it and a substantial share still undecided.

The result is a show floor that reflects a multi-powertrain aftermarket. Suppliers are preparing for EV and hybrid service while continuing to serve a very large installed base of aging internal-combustion vehicles.

What the 2025 Audience Says About 2026

Post-event feedback from AAPEX 2025 gives a useful indication of buyer priorities.

Among 906 surveyed attendees, 34% identified supply chain disruptions as a major challenge, followed by tariffs at 31% and attracting qualified employees at 27%. Exhibitors had a somewhat different perspective: among 110 respondents, attracting customers ranked first, followed by tariffs and market positioning.

The same attendee research also found that 86% said they achieved their objective of discovering new products, 81% achieved their objective of finding and evaluating new suppliers, and 92% met their relationship-maintenance objectives at the previous show.

Those figures help explain why AAPEX remains commercially important despite the availability of digital sourcing platforms. The value lies in compressing product comparison, supplier qualification, technical discussion and relationship building into the same three-day window.

AAPEX 2026 Looks Set to Be More About Decisions Than Discovery Alone

For automotive aftermarket suppliers and buyers, AAPEX 2026 arrives at a time when demand remains healthy but purchasing decisions are becoming more complicated.

An aging U.S. vehicle fleet supports replacement demand. At the same time, customers are more price-conscious, companies are reconsidering sourcing exposure, inventories require tighter management, and vehicle technology is raising the technical requirements of repair.

Against that background, the most relevant products in Las Vegas may not necessarily be the newest ones. Buyers are likely to pay close attention to suppliers that can demonstrate competitive total cost, dependable availability, consistent quality and credible technical support.

That makes AAPEX 2026 less a showcase of isolated automotive aftermarket products and more a concentrated view of how the industry's sourcing, distribution and service models are being adjusted for the next phase of the North American market.
