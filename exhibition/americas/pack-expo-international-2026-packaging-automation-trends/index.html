PACK EXPO International 2026: Packaging & Automation Trends

Aug 25, 2026

When PACK EXPO International returns to McCormick Place in Chicago from October 18 to 21, 2026, the event will arrive at a moment when packaging decisions are becoming more interconnected. Equipment buyers are not only comparing fillers, cartoners or palletizers. They are also weighing labor availability, retrofit economics, material reduction, extended producer responsibility, digital controls and the ability to keep existing assets productive for longer.

The biennial event functions as a broad sourcing and benchmarking platform for North American consumer packaged goods, food and beverage, life sciences and industrial manufacturers. Organizers currently expect more than 2,600 exhibitors across roughly 1.3 million net square feet, with approximately 48,000 professionals representing more than 40 vertical markets.

Scale Matters Because Packaging Projects Cross More Functions

The size of PACK EXPO International is useful because it reflects how packaging projects are actually purchased. Exhibitors span packaging machinery, automation and controls, materials and containers, food and beverage processing equipment, life sciences processing systems, packaging services and related supplies.

The attendee mix is similarly cross-functional. Plant managers, packaging engineers, operations directors, R&D teams, procurement professionals, quality specialists, sustainability teams, maintenance staff and corporate leadership are all part of the target audience. For suppliers, that creates a setting where technical evaluation and commercial discussion can happen within the same event. For buyers, it allows a project team to compare several parts of a line without treating each component as an isolated purchase.

Buyer Attention Is Concentrating on Line Efficiency and End-of-Line Automation

Previous PACK EXPO registration data gives a useful indication of what visitors are likely to search for in Chicago. Among packaging machinery categories, attendee interest has been strongest in bag and pouch filling and sealing, case and tray packing, labeling, material handling, palletizing and depalletizing, and robotics. Flexible packaging, cartons and boxes, containers and labels also rank among the most requested material categories.

That profile fits the investment signals reported by PMMI in its 2026 Packaging State of the Industry research. The association points to workforce development, knowledge retention, digital-tool adoption, retrofit strategies and asset preservation as continuing priorities. End-of-line automation, including robotic palletizers and autonomous mobile robots, is also receiving attention as manufacturers look for measurable productivity gains without rebuilding entire plants.

For visitors, the most productive conversations may focus less on isolated machine speed and more on integration, changeover, serviceability, controls, data visibility and the economics of upgrading an installed line.

The Show Floor Now Extends from Processing to Distribution

PACK EXPO International has increasingly organized its scale around application-specific zones. The 2026 edition includes dedicated destinations for food and beverage processing, logistics, healthcare packaging, containers and materials, package printing, confectionery and reusable packaging.

The structure reflects a supply chain in which packaging performance is evaluated beyond the primary package. The Food & Beverage Processing Zone covers mixing, batching, forming, hygienic design, inspection, thermal processing and plant engineering. The Logistics Pavilion moves downstream into warehousing, fulfillment, material handling, inventory control and distribution. Healthcare packaging adds requirements including sterile systems, serialization, track-and-trace, tamper evidence and cold-chain considerations.

For multinational buyers or contract manufacturers, this range can reduce the gap between process engineering, packaging engineering and distribution planning. It also gives component and technology suppliers exposure to adjacent applications that may not appear at narrower sector-specific exhibitions.

Sustainability Is Becoming an Equipment Specification Issue

Sustainability will be prominent in Chicago, but the more important signal is how the subject is being framed. PMMI reported in August 2026 that more than 82% of end users surveyed were actively engaged in sustainability adoption. Its industry research also highlights material reduction and extended producer responsibility pressures as factors influencing packaging decisions.

The 2026 education program reflects that shift. Sustainability Central is scheduled to cover recyclability, circularity, food-grade post-consumer recycled content, packaging footprint reduction and EPR. The Industry Speaks program also includes sessions on packaging policy, food labeling regulation and compliance.

Material changes can affect machinery settings, sealing performance, line speeds, inspection, secondary packaging and total system cost. Buyers evaluating recyclable structures or downgauged materials therefore have a reason to involve both packaging material suppliers and equipment engineers in the same discussion.

2024 Provides a Strong Benchmark, but 2026 Should Be Read on Its Own Terms

The previous Chicago edition established a high baseline. PACK EXPO International 2024 drew 48,000 attendees plus 29,500 exhibitor personnel, for total attendance of 77,500. A record 2,700 exhibitors occupied 1.32 million net square feet, and international attendance increased 19% compared with 2022.

Those figures matter for 2026 because they show that the event is not dependent on a single packaging segment or domestic visitor pool. The draw is broad enough to support cross-border supplier relationships and multinational sourcing activity. The show’s International Business Center and global supplier presence reinforce that role.

Current programming also points to a stronger emphasis on navigating the show efficiently. New Discovery Tours will guide qualified end users, contract packagers and retailers through selected technology themes, while the new SMB FastTrack program is intended to highlight exhibitors and education relevant to small and medium-sized businesses.

Education Topics Reveal the Questions Behind Capital Spending

More than 150 free educational sessions are planned across the Innovation Stage, Food & Beverage Processing Innovation Stage, Sustainability Central and Industry Speaks. The announced topics sit close to current capital-planning concerns.

Sessions include tariff and supply-chain volatility, return on investment for end-of-line automation, maintenance practices, digital manufacturing, AI-assisted programming, sustainable redesign, EPR and regulatory change. These issues influence when a project moves forward, how a specification is written and what level of flexibility a manufacturer expects from a new system.

For buyers with active projects, the educational program can serve as a filter for the exhibition floor. It helps identify which technologies deserve deeper evaluation and which market pressures may alter a project specification before purchase.

Why PACK EXPO International 2026 Deserves Industry Attention

PACK EXPO International’s main value is not a single dominant technology theme. Its strength is the concentration of suppliers, end users and technical disciplines across the packaging and processing chain.

For large manufacturers, Chicago offers a place to compare line-level investments across multiple plants and product categories. For smaller producers, the challenge is navigating a very large supplier base efficiently, which makes the new SMB and guided-tour formats relevant. For equipment makers, materials companies and automation suppliers, the show provides access to buyers whose requirements increasingly combine throughput, labor, compliance, sustainability and service support.

As the packaging market moves deeper into retrofit programs, automation upgrades, material transitions and regulatory preparation, PACK EXPO International 2026 should provide a useful reading of where capital spending is becoming more selective and where suppliers are adapting their portfolios. The most telling activity in Chicago may not be the number of machines running on the floor, but the types of problems buyers are asking those machines to solve.
