FABTECH 2026 Las Vegas: Metal Fabrication, Welding Automation and Manufacturing Trends

Apr 22, 2026

FABTECH 2026 will take place from October 21 to 23, 2026 in Las Vegas, Nevada, with official event materials positioning it as North America’s largest event for metal forming, fabricating, welding and finishing. Official FABTECH pages also note that the show is heading to Las Vegas for the first time in a decade, a detail that matters because location affects who attends, how buyers plan meetings and how broadly the event pulls from across the continent.

For suppliers and buyers alike, FABTECH is not simply another manufacturing exhibition. It sits at the intersection of several purchasing tracks that increasingly overlap on the plant floor: fabrication equipment, welding systems, automation, material handling, software, finishing and production engineering. That mix is what gives FABTECH its commercial weight. It is one of the few trade shows where a buyer can compare machine categories, automation strategies and process flow decisions in the same visit rather than across separate niche events. Official event materials also point to a conference program built around those adjacent technologies rather than a single process silo.

Why Las Vegas changes the profile of the event

FABTECH has long been associated with large U.S. industrial markets, but Las Vegas gives the 2026 edition a somewhat different role. According to FABTECH’s 2026 exhibit brochure, past Las Vegas editions drew 15% of attendees from outside the United States, while the top attending industries included aerospace, automotive, construction, energy, heavy equipment, military and defense, oil and gas and other transportation. That suggests a broader travel catchment than a strictly regional manufacturing show and helps explain why Las Vegas tends to appeal to companies planning multi-supplier meetings and cross-functional attendance.

That geographic draw is commercially important because metal fabrication sourcing is rarely limited to one department anymore. A machine shop may send operations leadership to evaluate throughput and labor savings, while engineering looks at process stability and compatibility and management focuses on payback and supplier support. The Las Vegas setting supports that kind of multi-role attendance. It is easier to justify as a national meeting point, especially for firms combining customer meetings, internal reviews and supplier shortlisting in a single trip. That is one reason FABTECH 2026 should attract attention beyond the usual Midwest fabrication base.

This is a buying floor, not just a branding event

The strongest argument for watching FABTECH 2026 is the audience profile from the most recent official post-event report. FABTECH’s 2024 report recorded 29,741 attendees, 1,531 exhibitors, 500+ speakers and 746,800 square feet of exhibition space. More importantly, 47% of attendees came from job shop or contract manufacturing companies, while 15% came from OEMs and 9% from suppliers. On the job function side, 28% were owners, company management or corporate executives and 16% were in manufacturing engineering or design engineering.

Those figures matter because they show that FABTECH is not dominated by casual traffic. The show attracts a mix of senior decision-makers and technical evaluators, with a large presence from companies that actively buy equipment or influence production investment. The same report also noted that 57% of attendees were first-time visitors, spent an average of 12.7 hours on the show floor and cited their top reasons for attending as evaluating new products, keeping up with industry trends and comparing products side by side. That is the profile of a serious sourcing event.

For exhibitors, the 2026 sales material reinforces that point from a different angle. FABTECH states that, based on past Las Vegas data, 93% of exhibitors were satisfied or very satisfied, while 83% would recommend the show to a colleague and 83% of attendees influence or approve equipment purchases. Those figures come from FABTECH’s own exhibitor-facing material, so they should be read as promotional statistics, but they still support the basic commercial case: FABTECH remains a lead generation and buyer access event with measurable purchasing relevance.

What exhibitors are likely to prioritize at FABTECH 2026

One of the most useful signals from FABTECH’s 2024 post-event report is the ranking of attendee technology interests. The leading areas were cutting at 43%, automation at 40%, welding machines at 40%, bending and forming at 39%, arc welding at 38%, lasers at 37% and robotics at 32%. Material handling and assembly also appeared prominently.

That mix says a lot about how the market is evolving. Buyers are still making core process decisions around cutting, forming and welding, but they are increasingly evaluating those assets in the context of labor efficiency, cell design and production flow. In practical terms, that means the biggest conversations at FABTECH 2026 are likely to center on robotic welding, automated part movement, laser productivity, operator support and integration between machine tools and upstream or downstream processes. The event’s exhibitor structure supports that view as well, with dedicated commercial categories spanning forming and fabricating, tube and pipe, finishing, stamping and press automation, welding and automation.

This matters because manufacturers are no longer comparing machines in isolation. A laser upgrade is often tied to material handling. A welding purchase is tied to staffing. A press brake discussion quickly turns into one about programming, repeatability and part flow. FABTECH works well because it lets buyers test those linked decisions in one place.

The wider automation market supports that reading

Recent automation data from the Association for Advancing Automation adds useful context. A3 reported that in Q1 2025, North American companies ordered 1,052 collaborative robots and cobots accounted for 11.6% of all robots ordered that quarter. Later, A3 said North American robot orders in Q3 2025 rose 11.6% year over year to 8,806 units. These are not FABTECH figures, but they help explain why automation occupies so much floor space and conference attention at the event.

For FABTECH attendees, the implication is straightforward. Automation demand remains active, but the emphasis is shifting toward flexible deployment, labor support and practical ROI rather than abstract digital transformation language. In metal fabrication, that usually shows up in robotic welding, machine tending, smaller automation cells, collaborative systems and process support tools that can fit mixed-volume production. Buyers attending FABTECH 2026 are likely to be asking not just what a system can do, but how quickly it can be deployed, staffed and justified.

Labor pressure still sits behind many equipment decisions

Another reason FABTECH 2026 deserves close attention is that the labor problem has not gone away. A 2024 Deloitte and Manufacturing Institute report found that U.S. manufacturing could need 3.8 million net new employees between 2024 and 2033, with about 1.9 million jobs potentially going unfilled if skill and applicant gaps persist.

That helps explain why FABTECH’s conference and educational messaging continues to tie technology to workforce issues. The event is not only about new machine launches. It is also about how manufacturers keep throughput up when experienced labor is limited, onboarding takes longer and process consistency matters more. In that environment, equipment categories such as welding automation, easier-to-program controls, inspection support and operator-assist systems become more attractive because they address staffing pressure as much as production speed.

The exhibitor mix tells its own story about the supply chain

FABTECH is especially useful because it reflects the structure of the industrial supply chain rather than a single narrow process segment. Official materials show the event supported by multiple industry organizations including AWS, FMA, SME, PMA and CCAI and the exhibitor categories stretch from welding and cutting to finishing and stamping. That breadth makes the show relevant to job shops, OEMs, contract manufacturers, integrators and plant engineering teams that need to connect several process steps rather than purchase one standalone asset.

The attendee list from the 2024 official report reinforces that point. The report says representatives came from companies such as Boeing, Caterpillar, Cummins, GE Aerospace, General Motors, Honda, John Deere, Lockheed Martin, NASA, Northrop Grumman, Nucor, Siemens, SpaceX, Toyota Motor North America and Yamaha Motor Manufacturing. No trade show attendee list proves purchase volume on its own, but it does show that FABTECH attracts interest from aerospace, transportation, heavy industry, defense and industrial equipment sectors at the same time.

What the recent editions suggest about 2026 demand

Official FABTECH news coverage described the 2025 Chicago show floor as a record-setting edition with more than 1,700 exhibitors across 885,000 square feet. Combined with the strong 2024 Orlando metrics, that suggests supplier demand for FABTECH remains firm and that exhibitors still see value in using the event for launches, lead capture and customer meetings.

For 2026, that likely means buyers can expect a floor shaped by three priorities. First, productivity gains from core fabrication equipment. Second, automation that addresses labor and consistency constraints. Third, process linking technologies that reduce bottlenecks between cutting, welding, forming, handling and finishing. Those themes fit both the official attendee interest data and the broader automation and workforce backdrop now shaping manufacturing investment in North America.

Why FABTECH 2026 is worth tracking

FABTECH 2026 matters because it sits at the point where equipment demand, labor constraints and process integration meet. It is large enough to serve as a market signal, broad enough to show how adjacent technologies are converging and practical enough to remain useful for buyers who need to compare machines, automation options and suppliers in person. With its return to Las Vegas, the 2026 edition should draw a wide audience across North American manufacturing and give a clearer read on where metal fabrication investment is heading next.
