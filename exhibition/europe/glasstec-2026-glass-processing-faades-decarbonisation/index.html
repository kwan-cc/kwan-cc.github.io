glasstec 2026: Glass Processing, Façades & Decarbonisation

Aug 25, 2026

When glasstec 2026 opens in Düsseldorf from 20 to 23 October, the most useful way to read the show will not be as a catalogue of glass products. It is better viewed as a snapshot of where capital spending, process improvement and product development are moving across the international glass value chain.

The event returns to Messe Düsseldorf with halls 10 to 17 assigned to the fair. Its scope covers glass production technology, processing and finishing, glass products and applications, tools and components, measurement and control systems, software, decarbonisation, consulting and research. That breadth matters because the sector's biggest commercial questions now cross traditional supplier boundaries. Furnace efficiency affects carbon costs, while software affects yield, maintenance and process stability.

For buyers and suppliers, glasstec 2026 is therefore less about one breakthrough product than about comparing how different parts of the supply chain are responding to the same pressures.

A meeting point for a fragmented glass value chain

glasstec's position in Düsseldorf is useful because the event brings machinery manufacturers, primary glass producers, processors, façade specialists, glaziers, architects, engineering firms and technology providers into the same venue. The official visitor profile also includes research institutions, universities and environmental and recycling specialists.

That structure supports cross-supply-chain purchasing. A processor evaluating a new tempering or insulating glass line may also be looking at quality inspection, software integration, edge processing, sealants or maintenance systems. A façade consultant may be comparing coated glass, laminated products, structural solutions and performance data rather than sourcing a single component.

By July 2026, the organiser reported that companies from 48 countries had announced participation. Final exhibitor totals should only be judged after the show, but the early geographic spread reinforces glasstec's role as a European event with a global supplier base.

What buyers are likely to examine most closely

The 2024 visitor data gives a useful indication of where attention will concentrate. Glass production and production technology attracted the highest stated product interest at 55%, followed by industrial glass processing and finishing at 37% and glass products and applications at 32%. Technical and functional glass drew 23%, while measurement, testing, control technology and software attracted 22%.

Those figures suggest that production equipment will remain central in 2026, but machinery will increasingly be evaluated together with automation, inspection, data and operating cost. Buyers are likely to compare not only throughput and cycle time, but also energy use, scrap reduction, predictive maintenance and the ability to connect equipment across production lines.

The official 2026 product categories reflect this buying logic, ranging from melting and production systems to cutting, grinding, coating, insulating glass, safety glass, laser processing, measurement technology and software.

Decarbonisation becomes an equipment question

Decarbonisation is one of glasstec 2026's three headline themes, alongside circularity and AI and digital technologies. The emphasis is commercially significant because glass manufacturing remains energy intensive, particularly at the melting stage. The European Commission continues to treat glass as an energy-intensive process industry and is examining pathways to carbon neutrality.

At the fair, this should push discussions beyond general sustainability claims. Buyers will be looking for evidence around furnace electrification or hybrid concepts, combustion efficiency, heat recovery, cullet use, process controls and the practical requirements of changing energy inputs. Suppliers able to connect emissions reduction with production reliability and operating cost are likely to have the more credible commercial conversation.

This is also why decarbonisation cannot be isolated from digitalisation. Better sensing, process control and production data are becoming part of the route to lower energy use and more consistent output.

AI moves closer to the production line

For 2026, glasstec has elevated AI into a specific focus within its AI and Digital Technologies theme. The organiser points to applications in process control, efficiency optimisation, maintenance and quality assurance.

The important question for industrial users will be where these systems deliver measurable value. Glass production and processing already generate data from furnaces, cutting tables, tempering lines, inspection systems and downstream handling. The opportunity is to use that information to detect defects earlier, adjust processes faster, schedule maintenance more intelligently and reduce losses between machines.

This makes software suppliers, machine builders and sensor companies increasingly interdependent. At glasstec 2026, the strongest digital propositions are likely to be those that fit existing production environments rather than requiring an entirely new automation architecture.

Circularity links production efficiency with material recovery

Circularity is the third main theme, but it is relevant to more than container glass recycling. In flat glass and façade applications, the discussion increasingly includes higher cullet use, design for disassembly, material separation, reuse and recovery from renovation and demolition projects.

The 2024 visitor survey showed 15% interest in glass recycling, a smaller share than core production technologies but large enough to indicate a defined specialist audience. For 2026, that topic sits closer to mainstream procurement because energy, raw materials and carbon performance are now linked.

The conference programme reinforces this direction through CircuClarity Two, glass trends sessions and the GLASS MELTING POT, while glass technology live will again connect university research with industrial applications and prototypes.

Façades and high-performance glazing keep downstream demand visible

glasstec is also important because it does not stop at the factory gate. Architectural glass, windows and façades bring end-use requirements back into the technology discussion.

Europe's building policy adds weight to that part of the show. Under the recast Energy Performance of Buildings Directive, zero-emission buildings become the standard for new public buildings from 2028 and for all new buildings from 2030. The Commission also identifies new windows as one measure used in energy renovation.

For glass suppliers, this keeps attention on insulating performance, solar control, coated products, vacuum insulating glass, façade integration and material efficiency. The 2026 architecture forum will focus on digital technologies and glass in climate-neutral architecture, while a new special show with ift Rosenheim will address resilient and security-critical glass for buildings and critical infrastructure.

What 2024 says about the commercial quality of the audience

The previous edition provides the clearest benchmark for 2026. glasstec 2024 recorded 1,257 exhibitors, 32,023 visitors and 60,379 square metres of net exhibition space. According to the official visitor structure data, 79% of trade visitors were decision-makers or involved in decision-making, while 31% cited identifying new suppliers or business partners as a reason for attending. Another 26% came to follow new developments and trends.

That combination is more important than the headline attendance alone. It suggests a show where technical discovery and supplier evaluation happen in parallel.

glasstec 2026 should be watched in the same way. The most telling signals will be which equipment categories attract investment attention, how suppliers quantify energy and material savings, how far AI has moved from demonstration to production use, and whether circularity is becoming part of mainstream specification. For companies active in glass manufacturing, processing, façade systems or industrial automation, Düsseldorf will offer a concentrated view of those decisions before they become visible in broader market statistics.
