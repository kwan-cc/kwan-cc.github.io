    Inside The Factory Gates: Victor Taichung’s Bold “Factory-as-Showroom” Adventure Ignites 2026 Momentum

    Mar 10, 2026

    In the crisp January air of Taichung, something quietly revolutionary unfolded.

    While much of the global machine tool industry was still digesting the good news from U.S.-Taiwan tariff talks—a favorable 15% rate without MFN stacking, signaling brighter days ahead—Victor Taichung (Taichung Precision Machinery) decided not to wait for the recovery to knock on its door. Instead, it opened its own doors wide.

    The Birth of a New Showcase Model

    On January 22–23, 2026, the company hosted its very first in-factory exhibition: the “2026 Smart Manufacturing & AI Supply Chain Alliance Showcase”. No crowded convention hall, no distant booths—this was the real thing. Twelve of the most advanced, battle-tested high-end intelligent CNC machine tools and all-electric injection molding systems hummed quietly on the production floor, surrounded by 25 key supplier partners who came not just to display, but to co-create.

    Over 100 major clients walked through those factory gates. Many didn’t leave empty-handed; orders were placed right there, amid the scent of coolant and the sound of precision spindles. For Victor Taichung, it was more than an event—it was a declaration of confidence and a new way of doing business.

    Chairman Huang Ming-Ho’s Three Pillars of Vision

    Chairman Huang Ming-Ho stood at the center of it all, welcoming guests with three clear reasons why this “factory-as-showroom” model mattered so deeply:

    1. After six years of operating their smart factory, the company had quietly built an arsenal of high-performance machines—5-axis powerhouses, mill-turn composites, ultra-responsive all-electric molding giants—that no traditional exhibition booth could ever fully demonstrate. Here, in the actual production environment, clients could watch real cycles, feel real rigidity, and envision real applications. The experience spoke louder than any brochure.
    2. Success in Taiwan’s machine tool world has never been a solo journey. Many of Victor’s suppliers are also its customers, and vice versa—a tight-knit ecosystem where everyone rises together. By inviting 25 partners to exhibit side-by-side, the company turned the event into a living symbol of solidarity. In tough times, “you in my mud, me in your mud” became more than a metaphor; it became strategy.
    3. Innovation never stops. The high-end 5-axis and turn-mill machines have already proven themselves rock-solid with demanding users. Mid-2026 will bring Taiwan’s largest all-electric injection molding machine yet (580-ton clamping force). And starting April, every new Victor machine carries a 3-year warranty—a bold move signaling that Taiwanese quality can stand shoulder-to-shoulder with Japanese benchmarks, while delivering faster service, lower ownership costs, and unbeatable value. Nothing convinces like seeing (and hearing) it in person.

    Trends on Display: Smart, Green, and Connected

    The two-day showcase wasn’t just machines on display. It wove together multi-axis machining, all-electric molding, digital twin technology, smart factory integration, and serious energy-saving & carbon-reduction solutions—aligning perfectly with where global manufacturing is heading. Two expert forums brought 10 supplier speakers to the stage, sharing the latest processing trends. The crowd listened intently; conversations flowed late into the evening.

    Voices from the Ecosystem: Five VVIP Partners Share Their Stories

    But the real heartbeat of the event came from five longtime “VVIP” partners who stepped forward to share their stories:

    • HIWIN Technologies Chairman Cho Wen-Heng recalled how, since 1989, HIWIN has trusted over 60 Victor machines—lathes, multi-axis, mill-turns, electric & hydraulic molders—running reliably day after day. He applauded the new 3-year warranty as a game-changer for Taiwan’s global image, and pledged HIWIN’s smart linear guides, ball screws, rotary tables, and upgrade kits to help Victor machines become even smarter and greener.
    • Delta Electronics Marketing Manager Lee Chia-Chun described a deep, two-way partnership: over 300 Victor machines across Delta’s factories, plus Delta controllers powering Victor’s injection systems. With AI driving explosive demand for high-performance cooling fans, the two companies are now co-developing ultra-responsive thin-blade molding machines—already mold-validated and poised for wider thin-wall applications.
    • Grundfos Business Manager Chen Su-Fang spoke of 30+ years of trust. The world’s No.1 pump maker (Denmark-founded, 20,000+ employees) relies on Victor machines in Denmark, Suzhou, and Miaoli plants alike. “Grundfos only chooses the best,” she said, and now looks to deepen collaboration on energy-efficient variable-frequency pumps, system integration, and smart manufacturing training.
    • Shin-Yain Industrial (SYIC) Chairman Chen Pen-Hung shared a personal history: his student internship decades ago, lifelong bonds with Victor’s third-generation leaders, and now nearly 50 high-end Victor machines across four factories since moving to Cingshuei in 2009. Ultra-high-pressure Vturn-S lathes (up to 140 BAR) and German-rivaling 5-axis centers have become the company’s “golden hens.”
    • AUTOGRIP Machinery Vice GM Chen Hsuan-Yu highlighted 30+ years of clamping system expertise. AUTOGRIP depends on Victor CNC lathes and machining centers for stable, automated production of power chucks and rotary cylinders. Together, they build complementary smart lines, exchange technology, and aim to conquer global markets side by side.

    As the final guests left on January 23, the factory quietly returned to its rhythm—but something had shifted. Victor Taichung hadn’t just hosted an exhibition; it had rewritten the playbook. In an era craving authenticity, real-world proof, and collaborative strength, the most powerful showroom may no longer be a distant hall. It may be the factory itself.

    For Taiwan’s machine tool and smart manufacturing community, this was more than a two-day event. It was the start of a story—one where technology, trust, and shared ambition write the next chapter together.

    (Adapted from original reporting by Zhuang Fuan / Commercial Times, January 28, 2026)
