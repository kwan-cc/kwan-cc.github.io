Navigating The US-Taiwan Tariff Deal: Relief for Semiconductor Procurement in 2026

The 2025 US tariff hikes under Section 232 rattled global supply chains, stacking 25-32% duties on Taiwanese goods and inflating procurement costs for US buyers by up to 20% on average. Taiwan's exports to the US—worth over $100B annually, with semiconductors dominating—faced disruptions, forcing owners and procurement teams to scramble for alternatives amid lead time delays and price surges. But January 2026's breakthrough US-Taiwan trade agreement caps tariffs at 15% (non-stackable on MFN rates) and offers semiconductor-specific exemptions, slashing landed costs while tying relief to $250B+ in Taiwanese investments into US fabs. This shift stabilizes pricing but introduces new dynamics for sourcing advanced nodes like 3nm/2nm chips.
