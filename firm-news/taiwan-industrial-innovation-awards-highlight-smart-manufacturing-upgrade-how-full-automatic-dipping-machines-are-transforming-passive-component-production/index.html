    Taiwan Industrial Innovation Awards Highlight Smart Manufacturing Upgrade – How Full Automatic Dipping Machines Are Transforming Passive Component Production

    Mar 19, 2026

    Manufacturers across electronics, passive components, and precision industries are accelerating automation and process innovation to remain competitive in global supply chains.

    At the 7th Industrial Innovation Awards organized by the Taichung Industry Innovation Association, 21 companies were recognized for outstanding achievements in product, technology, and service innovation, highlighting the increasing importance of advanced manufacturing capabilities in Taiwan’s industrial ecosystem.

    Among the awarded companies was LONG Automatic Machinery, a manufacturer specializing in automated dipping equipment used in passive component production processes.

    For procurement managers, factory owners, and manufacturing engineers, the message behind these awards is clear:

    Automation, process stability, and production efficiency are becoming the key differentiators in global electronics manufacturing.

    Why Automation Is Becoming Essential in Passive Component Manufacturing

    Passive components such as MLCCs, chip resistors, and inductors require extremely precise manufacturing steps, especially in termination and dipping processes.

    Traditional semi-automatic or manual dipping methods often create several operational challenges:

    • Inconsistent dipping depth
    • Uneven coating thickness
    • Low throughput
    • High labor dependency
    • Difficulty maintaining quality consistency at scale

    As global demand for electronic components increases, these challenges directly impact yield rate, quality stability, and production capacity.

    This is why many manufacturers are upgrading to full automatic dipping machine systems.

    Key Benefits of Full Automatic Dipping Machines

    A modern full automatic dipping machine integrates motion control, automated carriers, precision dipping control, and production line integration.

    Below is a comparison of typical production improvements.

    Manufacturing Factor Conventional Dipping Process Full Automatic Dipping Machine
    Production speed Limited by manual operation Continuous automated production
    Coating consistency Operator dependent Highly repeatable precision control
    Labor requirements High Reduced manpower
    Yield rate Variable Stable and predictable
    Process integration Stand-alone operation Integrated with automated production lines

    For high-volume electronic component manufacturing, automation helps factories achieve:

    • Higher throughput
    • Reduced defect rates
    • Lower long-term production costs
    • Better process repeatability

    Industry Trend: Smart Manufacturing in Traditional Industries

    During the award ceremony, industry leaders emphasized that traditional industries and SMEs are entering a critical stage of industrial upgrading, where innovation and automation will determine long-term competitiveness.

    This transformation is particularly visible in sectors such as:

    • Passive component manufacturing
    • Electronics assembly
    • Precision machinery
    • Semiconductor supply chain equipment

    Companies that invest in automation equipment, intelligent manufacturing, and process optimization are gaining a stronger position in global supply chains.

    How Automated Dipping Systems Improve Passive Component Production

    In passive component manufacturing, the dipping process is critical for forming reliable electrical connections.

    A full automatic dipping machine improves several production parameters:

    1. Precision Dipping Control

    Advanced motion systems ensure stable dipping depth and coating consistency.

    2. Continuous Production Flow

    Automated carrier systems enable non-stop production, minimizing downtime.

    3. Reduced Human Error

    Automation eliminates variability caused by manual operations.

    4. Higher Production Capacity

    Manufacturers can scale production without proportional increases in labor.

    These advantages are especially important for high-volume production of MLCCs, chip resistors, and inductors, where even minor process deviations can affect performance and reliability.
