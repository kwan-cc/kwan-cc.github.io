Types of Vacuum Fittings in The Semiconductor Industry: CF, KF, And ISO Flanges Compared

Dec 02, 2025

In the fast-paced world of semiconductor manufacturing, vacuum fittings play a crucial role in maintaining leak-free, high-purity environments essential for processes like lithography, etching, and deposition. These components ensure ultra-clean operations under extreme pressures and temperatures, directly impacting yield rates and defect reduction. As the semiconductor industry evolves toward smaller nodes (e.g., 2nm and beyond) and integrates technologies like EUV lithography, the demand for reliable vacuum flanges has surged. According to recent market analyses, the global vacuum components market for semiconductors is projected to reach $3.66 billion by 2025, growing at a CAGR of around 5-6%, driven by advancements in AI, 5G, and electric vehicles. This guide explores the key types of vacuum fittings—CF, KF, and ISO flanges—their applications in semiconductors, a detailed comparison, and emerging trends.

CF Flanges: Precision for Ultra-High Vacuum (UHV) Systems

CF (ConFlat) flanges are the gold standard for ultra-high vacuum applications in the semiconductor industry, where pressures as low as 10⁻¹² mbar are required to minimize contamination. Constructed from durable stainless steel (typically 304 or 316 grades), they use oxygen-free copper gaskets that form a knife-edge seal, ensuring exceptional airtightness.

Key Features and Benefits

  • Pressure Rating: Up to 10⁻¹² mbar, ideal for UHV environments.
  • Temperature Resistance: Withstand bake-out temperatures up to 450°C, supporting high-heat processes like annealing.
  • Durability: Metal-to-metal seals provide long-term reliability in harsh conditions.

In semiconductor fabs, CF flanges are commonly used in particle accelerators, molecular beam epitaxy (MBE) systems, and EUV lithography tools. For example, TSMC's advanced EUV setups employ CF DN200 flanges to achieve pressures below 10⁻¹⁰ mbar, reducing defect rates by nearly 30%. However, their bolted assembly can be time-consuming, making them less suitable for frequent maintenance.

KF Flanges: Quick and Efficient for Mid-Range Vacuum Needs

KF (Klein Flansche or Quick Flange) fittings prioritize speed and simplicity, making them a favorite in semiconductor labs and testing environments where ultra-high vacuum isn't mandatory. These flanges support pressures up to 10⁻⁸ mbar and are designed for rapid assembly without tools.

Key Features and Benefits

  • Ease of Use: A tapered clamp secures the flanges with an elastomer O-ring, allowing quick disassembly—perfect for iterative testing.
  • Size Range: Compact designs (e.g., DN16 to DN50) fit analytical instruments like mass spectrometers.
  • Cost-Effectiveness: Lower upfront costs compared to CF, with good performance in foreline systems.

In the semiconductor sector, KF flanges are ideal for metrology labs and pump-down cycles, such as TSMC's DN40 setups for rapid testing. Their main limitation is thermal stability; O-rings cap bake-out at around 150°C, restricting use in high-temperature applications.

ISO Flanges: Versatile Solutions for Large-Scale and Moderate Vacuum Systems

ISO flanges bridge the gap for larger systems in semiconductor manufacturing, handling moderate vacuums up to 10⁻⁸ mbar across sizes from DN63 to DN630. They come in two variants: ISO-K (claw-clamp for quick setup) and ISO-F (bolted for vibration resistance).

Key Features and Benefits

  • Flexibility: Suitable for atmospheric-to-high-vacuum transitions in chemical vapor deposition (CVD) reactors and space simulation chambers.
  • Scalability: Larger diameters support industrial-scale equipment like synchrotron beamlines.
  • Vibration Tolerance: ISO-F's bolted design excels in dynamic environments.

These flanges are widely used in semiconductor processes requiring balance between size and performance, such as wafer handling in fabs. While not as specialized as CF for UHV or as nimble as KF, ISO flanges offer robust all-around utility.

This vacuum flanges comparison highlights how CF excels in precision-critical UHV setups, while KF and ISO prioritize efficiency and scale, respectively. For instance, in high-vibration fabs, ISO-F's bolted design reduces downtime compared to KF's clamps.

Emerging Trends in Vacuum Fittings for the Semiconductor Industry

The semiconductor vacuum fittings market is evolving rapidly, with projections showing growth to $2.5 billion by 2033, fueled by biopharma and aerospace crossovers. Key trends include:

  • AI Integration: Innovations like AI-powered cleanroom nozzles that adjust parameters in real-time for wafer processing, enhancing precision and reducing defects.
  • Sustainability Focus: Shift toward eco-friendly materials and energy-efficient designs to meet ESG standards in fabs.
  • Regional Growth: China dominates with a 46% market share in vacuum connectors, driven by fabs from TSMC and SMIC.
  • High-Tech Events: At SEMICON Europa 2025, companies like Pfeiffer Vacuum showcased solutions for entire fab ecosystems, from cleanrooms to exhaust systems.

As the industry pushes for 1nm nodes and quantum computing, expect increased adoption of hybrid fittings combining CF's reliability with KF's speed.

Choosing the Right Vacuum Fitting for Your Semiconductor Application

Selecting between CF, KF, or ISO flanges depends on your setup:

  • UHV Priority? Opt for CF to ensure minimal contamination.
  • Frequent Access Needed? Choose KF for quick operations.
  • Large Systems? Go with ISO for versatility.
