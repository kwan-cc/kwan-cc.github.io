Defense and Military: Hydraulic Systems for Vehicles and Ground Support Equipment

Aug 18, 2026

Military vehicles and ground support equipment operate under strict limits on space, weight, heat and maintenance access. Their hydraulic systems must perform reliably within the platform’s actual duty cycle while remaining supportable throughout a long service life.

AnyPower develops custom servo hydraulic systems for non-weapon applications in military vehicles and ground support equipment.

Made in Taiwan. Engineered around the application. Designed for long-term support.

More Than a Hydraulic Power Unit

A hydraulic power unit may represent only a small part of a vehicle’s total cost. Its effect on platform availability can be much greater.

An oversized, poorly documented or difficult-to-replace hydraulic unit may create problems later in the platform lifecycle:

  • Reduced payload and installation space.
  • Higher heat generation inside enclosed compartments.
  • Additional cooling requirements.
  • More difficult maintenance and replacement.
  • Uncertainty about component origin and batch history.
  • Limited availability of spare parts.

For this reason, defense procurement involves more than comparing quotations. Customers also need to understand where the product comes from, how its configuration is controlled and whether the supplier can support the platform over time.

The U.S. Defense Logistics Agency identifies supply-chain traceability and approved-source documentation as relevant requirements in certain procurement contexts. Depending on the procurement, documentation may include the manufacturing source, part identification, supply-chain intermediaries and available batch, lot or serial-number information.

Requirements vary by country, contracting authority and program. The underlying principle is consistent: visibility into the product and its supply chain helps reduce lifecycle risk.

Designed for Mobile Platforms

Military vehicles and ground support equipment may operate in extreme temperatures, sustained vibration, shock, dust, moisture, vehicle inclination and intermittent high-load conditions.

They may also have limited installation space, restricted maintenance access and no practical way to dissipate heat like an open industrial machine.

A standard catalogue unit may meet a nominal pressure and flow requirement while still being unsuitable for the vehicle. The complete system must be considered together:

  • Hydraulic power.
  • Duty cycle.
  • Reservoir volume.
  • Thermal balance.
  • Available electrical or mechanical power.
  • Installation envelope.
  • Control strategy.
  • Maintenance access.
  • Environmental conditions.

AnyPower starts with the actual actuation requirement rather than selecting a standard unit first.

The design process begins with the load, stroke, actuation time, operating frequency, pressure, flow and available installation space. The hydraulic circuit is then calculated around the platform’s real operating conditions.

This approach helps prevent the use of a larger hydraulic unit than the application requires.

Smaller by Engineering

Compact design is not simply a matter of reducing the dimensions of a standard hydraulic power unit.

The pump, motor, control circuit, accumulator, reservoir and thermal-management strategy must be considered as one system.

Pressure When It Is Needed

For applications with intermittent hydraulic demand, accumulator-based pressure holding may reduce the need for continuous pump operation.

Depending on the load profile and system configuration, this can help reduce standby operation, relief-valve losses, heat generation and operating noise.

The final pressure-holding strategy is determined by the application’s load requirements, safety conditions and duty cycle.

Flow According to Demand

AnyPower’s servo hydraulic approach adjusts pump output according to the required operating condition.

Instead of continuously generating excess flow and dissipating it through a relief valve, the system is designed to respond more closely to the actual demand.

This approach can support:

  • Lower standby power consumption.
  • Reduced heat generation.
  • More controlled actuator speed.
  • Lower operating noise.
  • More efficient use of available electrical or engine power.

Actual performance depends on the system configuration and duty cycle. These factors are evaluated during the engineering assessment.

Space Returned to the Vehicle

Reservoir volume is calculated according to the actual circulating requirement, thermal balance, air separation and maintenance needs.

The goal is not to make the reservoir as small as possible. The goal is to avoid carrying unnecessary volume, weight and cooling demand onto the platform.

Hydraulic tank and vehicle-footprint reduction are also identified as relevant considerations in Parker’s defense-vehicle material.

Aviation Ground Support Equipment

Hydraulic ground support equipment is used for aircraft maintenance, hydraulic testing and servicing.

Military and commercial aircraft ground-support-equipment suppliers offer portable hydraulic test stands and other hydraulic test systems for maintenance and servicing applications.

Hydraulic test equipment can provide hydraulic power for functional checks, pressure and flow testing, leakage checks and maintenance procedures without operating the aircraft’s engines.

Potential applications include:

  • Mobile hydraulic power units.
  • Hydraulic test carts.
  • Maintenance platforms.
  • Aircraft component-handling equipment.
  • Ground-service equipment.
  • Hydraulic support systems.

Suitability for a specific aviation application must be assessed against the required pressure, hydraulic fluid, cleanliness, safety and qualification requirements.

Traceability as Part of Lifecycle Support

In a defense program, documentation is part of the product and part of the support relationship.

AnyPower can provide documentation according to the customer’s project and procurement requirements, including:

  • Country-of-origin statements.
  • Batch or lot records.
  • Serial-number information, where applicable.
  • Factory inspection records.
  • Certificates and conformity documentation, where applicable.
  • Product configuration information.
  • Replacement and service documentation.

This information can support supplier review, configuration control, nonconformance investigation and future replacement planning.

Supply-chain traceability is also relevant to counterfeit-part prevention. U.S. government research has identified counterfeit parts as a risk that can disrupt defense supply chains, delay missions and endanger service personnel.

The documentation package is defined according to the customer’s platform, procurement process and contractual requirements. AnyPower does not claim that one documentation package automatically satisfies every defense program.
