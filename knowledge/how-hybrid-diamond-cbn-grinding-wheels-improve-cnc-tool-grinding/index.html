How Hybrid Diamond-CBN Grinding Wheels Improve CNC Tool Grinding

Aug 21, 2026

Introduction

Manufacturers of carbide cutting tools are under constant pressure to increase productivity without sacrificing edge quality, dimensional accuracy, or wheel life. This challenge becomes more demanding when a five-axis CNC tool grinder must produce deep flutes, complex geometries, and consistent results across large production runs.

Hybrid Diamond-CBN grinding wheels offer one approach to this challenge. By combining Diamond and cubic boron nitride (CBN) abrasives with multiple bonding technologies, hybrid wheels are designed to balance cutting force, heat control, wheel strength, and profile retention. For carbide end mills and other precision tools, this combination can support more stable and efficient flute grinding.

Why Flute Grinding Is Demanding

Flute grinding removes a significant amount of material while creating a precise helical geometry. The grinding wheel must maintain its profile as it works through tough and brittle materials, often at high spindle speeds and under demanding feed conditions.

Common production challenges include:

  • Maintaining the correct flute profile and radius.

  • Controlling heat in the tool and grinding zone.

  • Preventing premature wheel wear.

  • Reducing the frequency of dressing or sharpening.

  • Achieving consistent results during unattended production.

  • Balancing material removal rate with surface and edge quality.

A wheel that cuts aggressively but loses its shape too quickly can create dimensional variation. A wheel with excellent shape retention but insufficient cutting ability can reduce throughput and increase spindle load. The objective is not simply maximum hardness; it is a controlled balance of grinding performance and process stability.

What Is a Hybrid Diamond-CBN Wheel?

A hybrid Diamond-CBN grinding wheel combines two superabrasive technologies in one wheel design. Diamond is widely used for nonferrous, hard, and dense materials such as cemented carbide, ceramics, glass, quartz, and graphite. CBN is particularly effective for hard-to-grind ferrous materials, including hardened steels and high-speed steels.

The abrasive combination is supported by a hybrid bonding method. Depending on the wheel design, the bonding system can be engineered to provide high bond strength, heat resistance, heat dissipation, and improved shape retention.

For CNC tool grinding, the benefit is a wheel designed around the complete grinding operation rather than a single abrasive characteristic. The result can be a useful combination of cutting power, durability, thermal control, and profile stability.

Benefits in CNC Tool Grinding

1. Higher Material-Removal Capability

A hybrid wheel can provide strong cutting action during flute grinding. This is valuable when machining carbide end mills or removing material from deep flutes. Besdia states that its XM Hybrid Bond wheel is designed for high-performance fluting and that a single feed can exceed 3 mm under suitable process conditions.

Actual performance depends on the tool material, wheel specification, machine rigidity, spindle speed, feed rate, coolant, and grinding depth. These parameters should always be validated through application testing rather than transferred directly from one machine to another.

2. Better Wheel Shape Retention

Flute geometry directly affects tool performance. If the wheel profile changes during grinding, the tool may show variations in flute form, core diameter, edge preparation, or clearance geometry.

A hybrid bonding system can help retain the wheel shape during extended grinding. This is especially important for production environments that require repeatability across many tools. Improved shape retention may also reduce the need for frequent wheel correction or dressing.

3. Improved Heat Management

Heat is a major concern in carbide tool grinding. Excessive heat can damage the tool surface, affect edge quality, or contribute to grinding defects. A wheel with good heat dissipation and heat resistance can help manage the grinding zone when paired with appropriate coolant delivery and process parameters.

Wheel design alone cannot eliminate thermal damage. Coolant flow, nozzle position, wheel speed, feed rate, depth of cut, and machine condition remain critical factors.

4. Longer Production Intervals

When a wheel maintains its cutting ability and profile over a longer period, operators may be able to extend the interval between dressing, sharpening, or wheel changes. This can support more continuous production and reduce non-cutting time.

Besdia describes its XM Hybrid Bond wheel as suitable for long-term unattended processing. Whether unattended operation is appropriate should be determined by the manufacturer’s process controls, in-process inspection, machine monitoring, and safety procedures.

5. Lower Total Tooling Cost

The purchase price of a grinding wheel is only one part of its cost. A more useful evaluation includes:

  • Number of tools produced per wheel.

  • Dressing and correction frequency.

  • Grinding cycle time.

  • Wheel-change downtime.

  • Scrap and rework rate.

  • Spindle power consumption.

  • Operator intervention.

A wheel that offers a stable profile and longer working life may lower cost per tool even when its initial purchase price is higher. Production trials should measure total cost per tool instead of comparing wheel prices alone.

Typical Applications

Hybrid Diamond-CBN wheels are particularly relevant to:

  • Five-axis CNC tool grinding.

  • Carbide end mill flute grinding.

  • Deep-flute machining.

  • Complex cutting-tool geometries.

  • High-volume tool production.

  • Tool regrinding where profile consistency is important.

  • Grinding of selected HSS and heat-treated materials.

Besdia identifies its XM Hybrid Bond wheel primarily for five-axis CNC tool grinding and fluting. The company also states that its hybrid wheels can be used for other tough and fragile materials, as well as HSS and heat-treated materials. The correct abrasive arrangement and specification should be selected according to the workpiece material and grinding operation.

How to Select a Hybrid Wheel

Before selecting a wheel, manufacturers should define the following requirements:

Workpiece Material

Specify whether the tool is made from tungsten carbide, HSS, a heat-treated material, ceramic, or another hard material. Material grade and hardness can influence the appropriate abrasive and bond specification.

Tool Geometry

Record tool diameter, flute length, helix angle, core geometry, flute depth, and edge preparation. Deep fluting and small-diameter tools can place different demands on wheel strength and profile control.

Machine Platform

Confirm the machine brand, spindle power, maximum speed, wheel dimensions, mounting interface, and available coolant system. A wheel must be compatible with the machine’s mechanical and safety requirements.

Production Target

Define the required cycle time, batch size, dressing interval, and unattended-production objective. These factors help determine whether the priority is cutting force, surface finish, wheel life, or dimensional consistency.

Quality Requirements

Establish acceptable limits for flute profile, runout, surface roughness, edge condition, and tool life. A grinding wheel should be evaluated against the final cutting-tool requirements, not only against its grinding speed.

Example of a Production Evaluation

Besdia’s published test data for an XM Hybrid Bond wheel reports testing on a Walter Helitronic Vision 400L machine using a 125 mm wheel for a 10 mm tungsten-carbide tool. The reported test included 650 tools, a 35-degree helix angle, a 1.85 mm flute grinding depth in a single feed, and a spindle speed of 2,520 rpm.

These figures are useful as a reference example, not as a universal production specification. A customer evaluation should repeat the test using its own tool material, machine, coolant, geometry, and inspection criteria. Results should include cycle time, wheel wear, spindle load, profile accuracy, surface quality, and the number of tools produced before dressing or replacement.

Practical Evaluation Checklist

When comparing a hybrid wheel with an existing Diamond or CBN wheel, record:

  1. Grinding cycle time per tool.

  2. Number of tools produced before dressing.

  3. Wheel runout before and after grinding.

  4. Flute profile and radius accuracy.

  5. Surface roughness and edge condition.

  6. Spindle load and peak torque.

  7. Coolant temperature and flow condition.

  8. Wheel consumption per production batch.

  9. Scrap and rework rate.

  10. Total cost per finished tool.

This data provides a more reliable basis for selection than a general claim about wheel hardness or durability.

Conclusion

Hybrid Diamond-CBN grinding wheels are designed to address several competing requirements in CNC tool grinding: strong cutting action, heat management, wheel strength, profile retention, and longer production intervals. These characteristics make them especially relevant to five-axis CNC flute grinding and carbide end mill manufacturing.

For manufacturers, the most effective approach is to evaluate the wheel as part of the complete process. Machine capability, tool material, geometry, coolant, grinding parameters, dressing strategy, and inspection must all be considered. When these factors are matched correctly, a hybrid wheel can help improve productivity, process stability, and total cost per tool.
